Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am writing to submit the manuscript "kfre: An R Package for Kidney Failure
Risk Estimation Using the Kidney Failure Risk Equation" for consideration in
The R Journal.

The Kidney Failure Risk Equation (KFRE) is the leading validated tool for
predicting progression to kidney failure in patients with chronic kidney disease
(CKD). It has been validated in over 700,000 individuals across 30 countries,
is formally recommended in the 2024 KDIGO CKD Clinical Practice Guidelines, and
has been incorporated into electronic health record systems internationally. The
clinical research community's need for a reliable, reproducible R implementation
of the KFRE is therefore both large and concrete.

Prior to the kfre package, R users wishing to compute KFRE estimates were
required to implement the published equations manually --- a process prone to
transcription errors, inconsistency across projects, and duplication of effort.
The kfre package addresses this directly by providing: (1) vectorized
predictions across all six KFRE model variants (4, 6, and 8 variables at 2-
and 5-year horizons) for both North American and non-North American populations;
(2) a data-frame wrapper that appends risk columns using a consistent naming
convention; (3) an R6 class interface for pipeline and application developers;
(4) CKD staging and ESRD outcome classification utilities; (5) urine
protein-to-creatinine ratio conversion based on published calibration
equations; and (6) publication-quality ROC and precision-recall curve plotting.

The paper is appropriate for The R Journal's "Packages" article type. It focuses
on software design decisions rather than nephrology methodology, provides a
fully reproducible worked example using synthetic patient data, and discusses
important limitations (population calibration, competing risks, measurement
conversion uncertainty) that users need to understand before clinical
deployment.

The package is available on CRAN (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=kfre),
passes all CRAN checks, includes a full test suite via testthat, and has a
pkgdown documentation site at https://lshpaner.github.io/kfre_r/. The source
code is hosted at https://github.com/lshpaner/kfre_r with an active issue
tracker. The paper and all code needed to reproduce it are included in the
submission. This manuscript has not been published or submitted elsewhere.

I hope the editors will find this contribution of value to the R community and
to the researchers and clinicians who use R in CKD research and care.
